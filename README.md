# Systems-Analysis-and-Design
## DriverPass System Design Portfolio

The DriverPass project involved designing a comprehensive software solution for a driving school owner named Liam. The client needed to transition from a manual, paper-based scheduling method to an automated, web-based management system. The goal was to design a platform that could handle online account registration, prevent double-booking of instructors and vehicles, manage user roles (Customer, Driver, IT, Management), and administer automated online practice tests.

## Strengths in the Design Process
I excelled at translating complex business requirements into clear, visual technical blueprints using UML diagrams. By carefully structuring the Use Case, Activity, Sequence, and Class diagrams, I was able to build a highly organized system architecture. Additionally, I successfully bridged the gap between technical design and business communication by translating these models into plain, jargon-free language for the client presentation.

## Areas for Revision
If I could revise one part of my work on these documents, I would expand the initial requirements-gathering phase to include more detailed input from the driving instructors and office staff. While the owner provided excellent high-level business goals, incorporating the perspectives of the daily end-users would have provided deeper insights into edge cases and granular workflow improvements, resulting in an even more robust Use Case diagram.

## Interpreting User Needs
I interpreted the user's needs by directly mapping Liam's specific pain points to functional system requirements. For example, his frustration with accidental double-bookings translated directly into an automated scheduling loop that queries fleet availability before confirming a reservation. It is critical to prioritize the user's needs during the design phase because a system is only successful if it actively solves the client's operational problems and is intuitive enough for their staff and customers to adopt.

## Future Approach to Software Design
My approach to designing software centers on thorough upfront analysis and object-oriented modeling. In future projects, I will continue to utilize UML diagramming as a foundational strategy to visualize database relationships and logical workflows before writing any code. Drawing on my focus in backend systems and data analytics, I will also prioritize early data modeling and ensure frequent stakeholder check-ins to maintain strict alignment between the technical architecture and the evolving business requirements.
